project_summary = """
This final-year research project investigated how small-molecule signaling could be used to better control early cardiac differentiation from human embryonic stem cells. Using a chemically defined E8-based system, I focused on how developmental signaling and culture timing could influence cardiomyocyte differentiation and subtype specification.

The study built on an early-passaging strategy designed to remodel cell adhesion and improve cardiac differentiation (<a href="https://doi.org/10.7150/ijbs.120853" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Liu et al., 2025</a>). Day 2 passaging produced more uniform cultures and stronger cardiac marker expression. From there, I participated in studies of chamber-specific cardiomyocyte differentiation and subtype-associated phenotypes using molecular and imaging-based characterization. Because this work remains unpublished and is associated with ongoing intellectual-property development, detailed signaling conditions and subtype-specific findings are not disclosed here. The project received approximately half a million MOP in government funding from the Science and Technology Development Fund (FDCT) of Macao SAR (Project No. CIE/HA/2026/0008)and has translational potential for disease modeling, drug screening, and future cell-based therapeutic applications.
